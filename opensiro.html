Cross-SLM Consensus Gate

Look for improvements
that survive a change of model.

Core hypothesis

One small model can reward model-specific behavior. OpenSiro instead asks whether the same harness change improves a heterogeneous set of inexpensive models.

Evidence from prior work

The cheap signals can work.
The exact gate still needs proof.

Published results

Existing studies support the ingredients behind OpenSiro: cheap proxies can preserve useful rankings, harness improvements can transfer across models, and SLM-first development can cut cost. They also show why frontier validation cannot be removed.

01 / Cheap proxy<1% cost

PACE preserves a useful evaluation signal.

Across 14 models and four agentic benchmarks, a compact proxy reached Spearman correlation above 0.80 and about 85% pairwise ranking accuracy at much less than 1% of full evaluation cost.

PACE · arXiv ↗Supports cheap screening under a different cross-model protocol.
02 / Harness transfer116 / 126

Life-Harness transfers beyond its source SLM.

A harness evolved only from Qwen3-4B-Instruct trajectories transferred to 17 other models and improved 116 of 126 model-environment settings across 18 backbones.

Life-Harness · arXiv ↗Supports reusable, model-external harness structure.
03 / SLM economics4% cost

Better harnesses make small models practical.

Automated harness adaptation improved 16 of 21 task-SLM pairs. The best result recovered 89.7% of LLM performance at 4% of the cost.

Better Harnesses, Smaller Models · arXiv ↗Supports the cheap development loop, not frontier prediction.
04 / Boundary conditionPartial transfer

One model is not a reliable surrogate.

HarnessDev found that evolution gains were unstable, transferred only partially to held-out tasks, and depended strongly on the model executing the harness.

HarnessDev · arXiv ↗This is the reason to test heterogeneous SLMs and keep the frontier model as the release judge.
What the literature supports

Cheap screening is plausible. Harness improvements can transfer, but transfer is not automatic.

What OpenSiro must establish

Consensus adds precision. Measure whether 5/5 SLM agreement retains frontier-positive commits better than any single SLM.

These results are evidence for the premises of multi-fidelity harness CI. None directly validates OpenSiro’s proposed Cross-SLM Consensus Gate; that conditional transfer probability is the research objective below.

Research objective

Does agreement buy
a useful frontier signal?

Must be measured
1 / 5→2 / 5→3 / 5→4 / 5→5 / 5P(frontier uplift | agreement)

The hypothesis does not require small-model scores to predict a frontier score. It succeeds if agreement rejects many commits while retaining most changes that would improve the frontier model.

Experiment

Freeze first. Reveal later.

  • 5 heterogeneous fixed SLM families
  • 1–3 benchmark-generator families
  • 50–100 real harness commits
  • held-out generated samples
  • 1 frontier model after SLM results are frozen
Primary measures

Measure the decision.

  • frontier transfer precision and recall
  • frontier evaluations avoided
  • cost per discovered frontier improvement
  • cross-model variance
  • false-positive and false-negative commits
