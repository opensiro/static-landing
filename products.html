vsmlite

Active · Canonical profile

The minimal parent–child autonomy lifecycle. One viable parent incubates one child until it can stand on its own.

vsmlite is the active minimal implementation profile of the VSM methodology. It narrows the problem to its smallest useful mechanism: one parent organization forms, hosts, supervises, and gradually matures a single child VSM.

The parent temporarily supplies the viability functions the child does not yet own — coordination, control, audit, intelligence, policy. Responsibility transfers as evidence accumulates, until the child operates autonomously inside an explicit boundary.

Why it comes first

A general organizational factory would require compilers, registries, type systems, and migration tooling before the smallest lifecycle is validated. vsmlite tests the central thesis directly: one lineage, inspectable state, evidence-based autonomy, reversible graduation.

Meaning of autonomy

Autonomy is not isolation. A child is autonomous when it preserves its mission and viability within an agreed boundary using its own complete S1–S5 structure — while remaining recursively nested and policy-aligned.

opensiro

In development · Benchmark platform

AI-native benchmarks where independent researchers prove their craft on open leaderboards — no conference funnel required.

opensiro is an AI-native benchmark platform. The premise: an independent researcher should be able to prove their ability on a public leaderboard instead of grinding through the A/A* conference funnel or collecting a Ph.D.

Standardized tasks, open runs, traces, metrics, leaderboards, and failure analysis — all in the open. Build a system, submit it, and let the scoreboard speak.

Neutral by design

The platform compares vsmlite-built organizations, manually authored systems, and non-VSM baselines without assuming any methodology is correct. Evidence flows back as transition selection.

Current state

Prototype and platform-design stage. Existing orchestration, sandbox, and trace components are inputs — not proof of one integrated deployment.
